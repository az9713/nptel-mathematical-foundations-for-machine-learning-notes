A study route through the mathematical foundations

The course connects three questions: what a representation can express, what observations justify believing, and how a fitted model is computed. Each question needs a different mathematical language. Learning their connections is more useful than memorizing an isolated catalogue of formulas.

Read in the source course's order

Chapters 1–10 follow the linear algebra lectures. They move from scalar arithmetic and vector spaces through bases, transformations, eigenstructure, orthogonality, projection, least squares, principal components, singular values, and margins. Treat the distinction between a vector and its coordinates as a recurring question: changing coordinates can change a matrix without changing the underlying transformation.

Chapters 11–18 follow probability and statistics. Begin with a specified sample space, then develop conditioning, random variables, distributions, moments, joint behavior, concentration, and sample geometry. Keep three different claims separate: a model's probability, an estimate from observations, and a finite-sample or asymptotic guarantee about that estimate.

Chapters 19–26 develop differentiation, matrix derivatives, optimization, gradient algorithms, networks, and learning applications. A derivative describes local change under assumptions. An update rule uses that description to make a finite move. A convergence theorem requires additional conditions. A low training objective is another proposition again, separate from future predictive performance.

Use each chapter as a mathematical investigation

Work through the opening example before reading the result. At a displayed equation, identify the domain of every object, check matrix shapes, and name the hypothesis used in the next step. Then reproduce the numerical check and change one assumption. Duplicate a feature column, remove centering, introduce dependence, or increase a step size beyond its bound. The resulting failure often explains why the hypothesis was needed.

Attempt the six problems before revealing their solutions. A complete solution should state the mechanism as well as the answer. For code, run the entire chapter's listings in order in a fresh Python process. Some later listings may reuse earlier definitions. Outputs in the notes come from the stated constructed examples; they are not measurements of a deployed learning system.

Four connections to revisit

ConnectionWhat to establishWhere to return
Projection and estimationLeast squares makes the residual orthogonal to the feature span. Conditional expectation has an analogous residual property under square integrability.Chapters 7–8 and 16
Covariance and geometryA sample covariance is a scaled Gram matrix of centered columns. Its eigenvectors describe variance directions under the chosen units.Chapters 9 and 18
Curvature and computationQuadratic curvature determines how each error direction changes under a gradient update. Ill-conditioning creates disparate time scales.Chapters 20–23
Chain rules and learningBackpropagation accumulates derivatives of a scalar loss through a composition of maps. It computes a gradient; it does not establish global optimality.Chapters 20–21 and 24

The projection analogy has a precise boundary: a finite data matrix describes observed coordinate vectors, while conditional expectation concerns random variables and an underlying probability law. A formal similarity between their orthogonality identities does not make their assumptions interchangeable.

A small project that exercises the whole course

Build a document relevance classifier using a deliberately small numerical representation. Record the representation and feasible inputs. Check rank and scaling, choose a split before fitting, and compare a linear score with logistic probabilities. Verify the analytic gradient with directional finite differences, inspect convergence as the step size changes, and report both classification errors and probability quality on untouched data.

Then remove one feature that appears unimportant to the leading principal component. Test whether label prediction deteriorates. This experiment separates reconstruction quality from task relevance. It also provides a concrete reason to study geometry, probability, and optimization together: each can succeed at its own objective while the final application fails.

Reading and evidence boundaries

This is an independent educational edition. The source map accounts for all 70 numbered lectures and the introduction, including the consolidation and final-summary recordings. Added proofs, examples, exercises, and code are teaching constructions rather than a verbatim account of the videos. Caption provenance, reference inspection, scientific review, executable checks, and browser checks have separate records.
