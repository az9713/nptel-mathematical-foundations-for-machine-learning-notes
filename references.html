Primary references and source boundaries

The recordings determine the chronological topic sequence. Mathematical reference texts and original research support specific extensions and checks; the notes supply their own explanations, examples, and exercises. A source link does not imply that every result in that source was independently verified.

Course sources

  • Mathematical Foundations for Machine Learning — NPTEL playlist, Ashok Rao and Arulalan Rajan. All 70 numbered lectures and the introduction are included. YouTube's displayed order is reversed; the book follows ascending lecture numbers.
  • Official NPTEL syllabus, course 106108702: course identity, instructor attribution, institutions, prerequisites, and mathematical scope.
  • Official course page: source course context. Enrollment dates and administrative details are historical rather than promises of current availability.

Mathematical references

  • Marc Peter Deisenroth, A. Aldo Faisal, and Cheng Soon Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning, Cambridge University Press, 2020; author-hosted draft dated 2024-01-15. Relevant printed sections and pages are identified beside chapter claims. Chapter 1 gives orientation; Chapters 2–7 cover core mathematical foundations; later chapters discuss learning applications. The PDF is linked and retained privately for inspection, not redistributed.
  • Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, Cambridge University Press, 2004. Used where chapter-specific citations identify inspected material on convexity, optimality, constraints, and algorithms. Convex results are not extended automatically to nonconvex neural-network objectives.

Limit-theorem reference

Scott Sheffield, MIT 18.175 Theory of Probability, Lecture 15, slides 10–11, Spring 2014: Lévy's continuity theorem and the classical central limit theorem under independent, identically distributed sampling with finite positive variance. These statements support the precisely bounded theorem in the probability chapters; an asymptotic limit is distinct from a universal finite-sample normal approximation.

Use chapter-specific references for a claim

Each chapter lists the sections, pages, documentation, or original research used for its actual arguments. Those local references take precedence over this orientation list. Source inspection establishes what the reference says at the cited location; numerical agreement establishes a particular implementation check. Neither constitutes formal verification of the entire source or a general empirical validation.
