Numerical laboratory: five connections across the course

These constructed experiments connect rank, variance, probability bounds, matrix derivatives, and stable optimization. They use exact or independently computed references to expose a particular mechanism. They are mathematical teaching constructions, not measurements of a deployed model.

Download numerical_lab.py and run python numerical_lab.py with NumPy installed. The script writes no files and makes no network requests. Each experiment returns its results after assertions pass. Small floating-point differences can occur across numerical libraries; the mathematical comparisons use tolerances.

1. Identical training predictions can hide different parameters

Take the three-row matrix whose columns are $(1,2,3)^\top$ and twice that vector. Its rank is one, because the second column adds no independent direction. Both weight vectors $(1,0)^\top$ and $(-1,1)^\top$ predict $(1,2,3)^\top$. Their difference belongs to the kernel of the data matrix.

The least-squares solver selects the minimum-norm weight vector $(0.2,0.4)^\top$ under its stated convention. To verify the selection independently, all exact fits satisfy $w_1+2w_2=1$. Substituting $w_2=t$ gives squared norm $5(t-0.4)^2+0.2$, minimized at $t=0.4$. At a new input $(1,3)$, the first two weight vectors predict $1$ and $2$. This disagreement is relevant only if that new input is feasible: the training column relation may or may not be a permanent physical constraint.

2. Preserving variance can discard the label

Use four observations $(\pm10,\pm1)$ containing every sign combination. They are centered. The two feature columns are orthogonal, with squared lengths $400$ and $4$. The leading principal component is therefore the first coordinate direction, and its share of total squared variation is $400/404=100/101$.

Now define the class label by the sign of the second coordinate. For each value of the retained first coordinate, there is one observation of each class. Every deterministic classifier using only that retained coordinate gets at most half the four labels correct. The discarded coordinate identifies every label in this constructed sample. Reconstruction quality and supervised relevance have different objectives.

3. A finite-sample bound and an asymptotic approximation are different objects

Let $K$ be the number of successes in 20 independent fair Bernoulli trials. Its sample proportion is $K/20$. Consider $|K/20-1/2|\ge0.3$, equivalently $|K-10|\ge6$. Exact enumeration sums the binomial masses for $K\le4$ and $K\ge16$. Chebyshev uses only the proportion's variance, $1/(4\cdot20)$, and divides it by $0.3^2$.

A normal approximation uses mean $10$ and variance $5$ for the count. With continuity correction, the two tail boundaries are $4.5$ and $15.5$, giving standardized distance $5.5/\sqrt5$. This approximation is not a distribution-free bound. Its disagreement with exact enumeration is observable in this small example.

4. A directional check tests a matrix derivative

For matrices $X$, $W$, and $Y$ with compatible dimensions, define $L(W)=\|XW-Y\|_F^2/2$, where the squared Frobenius norm sums the squared matrix entries. Expanding the differential gives $\nabla_WL=X^\top(XW-Y)$. A perturbation matrix $D$ predicts directional rate $\operatorname{tr}((\nabla_WL)^\top D)$, where trace sums diagonal entries.

The supplied script chooses explicit rectangular data and nonsymmetric parameters. It compares that expression with $[L(W+\epsilon D)-L(W-\epsilon D)]/(2\epsilon)$ at $\epsilon=10^{-5}$. The analytic value is approximately $-0.95$, while the central difference is approximately $-0.9499999999940111$. Their absolute discrepancy in the recorded run is about $5.99\times10^{-12}$. This numerical agreement checks one construction; the derivation supplies the general formula.

5. Curvature determines a stable step interval

Let $J(w)=w^\top Hw/2$ with $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,10)$ and start at $w_0=(1,1)^\top$. Gradient descent with step $\eta$ gives $w_{k+1}=(I-\eta H)w_k$. Each coordinate therefore changes by a scalar factor, $1-\eta$ or $1-10\eta$. Convergence from every starting point requires both factors to have magnitude below one, giving $0\lt\eta\lt0.2$.

After 20 updates, the norm is $0.12157665459056928$ at step $0.1$ and approximately $3325.256730081163$ at step $0.25$. The latter follows a gradient direction but takes unstable finite steps. These values agree with direct evaluation of $(1-\eta\lambda)^{20}$ for each eigenvalue $\lambda$. They do not establish an appropriate step for an unrelated nonquadratic model.

Experiments to try

Replace exact feature duplication by a small perturbation and inspect singular values as well as fitted coefficients. Rescale one PCA feature while leaving the class rule fixed. Increase the number of Bernoulli trials and compare exact tails with normal approximations at a fixed standardized threshold. Sweep finite-difference step sizes. Finally rotate the quadratic Hessian without changing its eigenvalues and check that the stable step interval remains the same.

Each change asks a distinct question. Near dependence is a conditioning problem; feature scaling changes geometry; tail approximation needs a probability model; finite differences mix approximation and rounding error; eigenvalues control a quadratic update's modes. The laboratory's assertions verify those constructed relationships and are not substitutes for the assumptions.
